WHY YOUR NUMBER WAS WRONG

Your export summed to over a crore a quarter. The helpdesk's own report says
around Rs 11 lakh. Both are reading the same data.

The legacy Freshdesk system stored refund amounts in its own native unit —
effectively paise, not rupees. When that data was migrated into the current
helpdesk, the raw numbers came across unchanged. So every legacy refund
appears 100 times larger in a raw export than it actually is.

Corrected figure: Rs 67 lakh total over the 18 months Jan 2025 – Jun 2026,
or roughly Rs 11 lakh per quarter. That matches Sameer's helpdesk figure.

Additionally, 638 tickets were imported twice during the migration. I removed
the duplicates before counting.


WHAT THE 18 MONTHS ACTUALLY LOOK LIKE

Total refunds:            Rs 67,09,932
Monthly average:          Rs 3,73,000
Most recent quarter run:  Rs 12,79,823 (Apr–Jun 2026)

Refunds are rising. The quarterly total has roughly doubled from Q1 FY2025
(Rs 6.1 lakh) to Q2 FY2026 (Rs 12.8 lakh). Priya is right that this tracks
the Q4 policy change on customer disputes — the trend is visible in the data.

dropdown, and agents default to it. That shows in the data.


THE SPECIFIC PROBLEM: POLICY IS NOT BEING ENFORCED

Your own support policy (§5) caps goodwill credits at Rs 500 per ticket and
requires Team Lead approval for anything over that amount.

Of 991 GW-OTHER refund tickets, 879 exceeded Rs 500.
Average GW-OTHER refund: Rs 2,933.
Total paid above the Rs 500 ceiling: Rs 24,32,132 over 18 months.
That is approximately Rs 4.6 lakh per quarter above the policy limit.

If the cap had been enforced, GW-OTHER spend would have been around
Rs 5 lakh total, not Rs 29 lakh.


WHERE THE MONEY IS GOING — BY AGENT

Returns Desk and Billing process the large majority of refunds, which is
expected by design — §6 says Returns Desk owns refund processing.

However, the highest GW-OTHER rates (i.e., proportion of refund tickets
that are coded as goodwill rather than a specific reason) are:

  Reyansh Mishra (Escalations & Warranty):  63% of his refund tickets
  Steven Ansari (Chat Frontline):            60%
  Anjali D'Souza (Chat Frontline):           59%
  Payal Bhatia (Chat Frontline):             55%

A high GW-OTHER rate is not necessarily misconduct. It may mean the agent
could not find the right code, or the resolution genuinely was goodwill.
But it does mean these tickets are hard to audit. The flag is for the
Team Lead to investigate, not Finance to act on directly.


THE OTHER FINDING: DOUBLE REMEDIES

Neha mentioned this and she was right. 166 tickets show both a refund raised
and a replacement issued on the same order. Policy §5 is explicit: a customer
cannot receive both. These need to be reviewed by the Returns Desk and Finance.
Total refund value on those 166 tickets: Rs 5,74,191.


THE BUSINESS GOAL, AS A NUMBER

Enforce the existing Rs 500 goodwill cap.

Current quarterly GW-OTHER excess above cap: Rs 4.6 lakh
Realistic target after enforcement: Rs 1.5 lakh/quarter
Estimated quarterly saving: Rs 3.1 lakh
Estimated annual saving: Rs 12.4 lakh

No new policy required. The rule is already written. It is not being
followed, and there is no automated check. A simple approval gate in the
helpdesk — requiring Team Lead sign-off before a GW-OTHER refund above
Rs 500 is processed — would capture most of this.
